Dirac notation and measurement probabilities

|ψ⟩ = (1/√2)|0⟩ + (1/√2)|1⟩

The bars below show probabilities in the computational basis. They do not show the complex phase directly.

Important: a measurement outcome is probabilistic. The amplitudes determine probabilities through their squared magnitudes; relative phase affects other measurement bases and interference.

1. Dirac (bra-ket) notation

A pure single-qubit state can be written as:

|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩

Here α and β are generally complex probability amplitudes. A valid normalized state satisfies:

|α|² + |β|² = 1

The computational-basis measurement probabilities are P(0)=|α|² and P(1)=|β|².

2. Circle / Bloch-sphere notation

For a pure qubit, a standard parameterisation is:

|ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + eⁱᵠ sin(θ/2)|1⟩

θ sets the polar angle and φ sets the relative phase/azimuth. The corresponding Bloch vector is:

x = sin θ cos φ
y = sin θ sin φ
z = cos θ

The Bloch sphere gives a geometric picture of a single-qubit pure state. It does not by itself visualise general multi-qubit entanglement.

3. Two-qubit example: Bell state

|Φ⁺⟩ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2

This state has two equally likely computational-basis outcomes, 00 and 11. It is entangled: it cannot be written as a product of two independent single-qubit states. A single Bloch sphere cannot fully represent this joint state, so this project displays its Dirac notation and measurement distribution separately.

Project title

Multi-qubit State Visualiser for Teaching

An interactive educational webpage that connects quantum state notation to a visual representation and checks common misconceptions through a short quiz.

Objectives

  • Present single-qubit states in Dirac notation.
  • Relate θ and φ to a Bloch-sphere representation.
  • Display computational-basis measurement probabilities.
  • Introduce a two-qubit Bell state and explain why it is not represented by one Bloch sphere.
  • Assess understanding with instant quiz feedback.

Tools and methodology

Tools: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, SVG.

Method: use the standard parameterisation of a pure qubit; compute amplitudes and probabilities; map the angles to a Bloch vector; display preset states; evaluate quiz responses.

Limitations

  • The Bloch-sphere drawing is a schematic 2D projection, not a full 3D interactive renderer.
  • Only selected two-qubit examples are included; the Bell state is not drawn as two separate Bloch vectors.
  • No quantum hardware or external libraries are used.

Conclusion

This prototype demonstrates how algebraic notation, measurement probabilities, and geometric intuition complement one another when learning quantum states. The two-qubit Bell-state example also highlights the boundary of the single-qubit Bloch-sphere picture.
